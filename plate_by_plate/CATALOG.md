# Plate by Plate

A conveyor-belt sushi bar for your desk. Plates glide past under glass domes; take one when you fancy it, eat it in three bites, and return the empty plates for a small prize. There is no timer and nothing to lose: leave it running and play with it when you like.

## What you see

- The belt: a dark bar with three lamps, the belt running from right to left, each plate under a clear dome, its rim in the colour of its price. The lamp in the middle marks the plate you can take.
- 23 dishes: nigiri with salmon, tuna, shrimp, sweet omelette, eel, squid and scallop; salmon roe and sea urchin; cucumber, tuna, big and inside-out rolls; a tofu pouch, a hand roll, edamame, miso soup, custard pudding, matcha ice and a slice of melon. Three rare ones come on gold plates, and a shimmer of gold runs along the belt before one arrives.
- A plate in front of you: the plate comes forward as warm light, the dome is lifted, and the dish stands in a gold glow with its name beside it. Three bites, a few grains flying, and a contented MM! with the count of the day, or NEW when it is the first of its kind.
- Your table, one floor lower: the empty plates in piles under a lamp, a red machine with a slot that takes them back and counts them, and five small lamps that fill up.
- Every fifth plate: a capsule drops, rocks and opens. The first one always holds a keepsake, later ones often do: a lucky cat, a daruma, a tea cup, a fish kite, a tiny chef, a lantern, a shrine gate, a mountain, a wooden doll, a cherry blossom. The newest four stand on your table in the light, the older ones on a shelf.
- The menu: a row of 23 small lights in the table's edge, one for each dish you have eaten. When all are lit there is a short ceremony, and a gold chopstick rest lies on the counter from then on.
- Lunch and dinner time are busy; late at night the bar is dim and fewer plates come by.

## Controls

- The controls by their names: Start is the big button on top, OK is a press of the knob, Back is the Back button.
- On the belt: Start or OK takes the plate in the light. Turn the knob to look down at your table. Back leaves the app.
- With a plate in front of you: Start or OK takes a bite; three bites and it is eaten. Back puts an untouched plate back on the belt.
- At your table: Start or OK pushes one plate into the slot. Turn the knob, or press Back, to go up to the belt again.
- Every start begins with the author's mark for a second and a half and opens on the main screen: the name over the running belt. Start enters at once, pressing the knob (OK) opens the guide, Back leaves. Left alone, the name fades after four seconds and the bar is open.
- The guide is five pages, one control a page, that you turn yourself: Start, OK or a turn of the knob forward for the next page, a turn back or Back for the page before. Any key on the last page, or Back on the first, closes it and leads back to the main screen. The first time you use the app it opens by itself.
- When you wait, a small light under Start or the knob shows what it does there.

## Settings

- Belt speed: slow, normal or fast.
- Quiet nights: from 11 pm to 6 am the bar is dimmer and fewer plates come by.
- Start over: flip this switch to clear everything the app has kept and see the guide again. Each flip starts over once. It cannot be undone.

## Data and privacy

- Kept on the bar, in the app's own storage: which dishes you have eaten, how many plates today and in all, the plates on your table, how many you have returned, your keepsakes, and that you have seen the guide.
- Nothing leaves the bar. The app uses no internet, no account and no key.
- All drawings are the author's own. The app is not affiliated with any restaurant, brand or game.
