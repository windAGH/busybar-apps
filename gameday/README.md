# Gameday

NFL game day on your BUSY Bar. One game at a time as a score strip: both teams, the score, the quarter and the clock, the timeouts each side has left, and along the bottom the field with the ball on it.

## What you see

- **During a game**: the two scores, the quarter over the game clock (now and then the down and distance, such as 2&7), three marks above each score for the timeouts left, and the field along the bottom row: an orange dot for the ball, a yellow line for the line to gain, and lights running over the ground already covered.
- **Big plays**: a sign in the team's colour, staged like a broadcast graphic, for a touchdown, a field goal, a safety, a two-point play, an interception, a fumble that changes hands and a sack. When the feed lists who made the play, the sign goes on to his number and name. Only for the game the app stays on.
- **Red zone**: when the ball comes within 15 yards of it, the last 20 yards of the field turn red and breathe.
- **Timeout**: T/O over a countdown, and the mark of the timeout just called blinks. **Halftime**: Half over a countdown to the second half. Both countdowns are estimates: the feed only says that a break is on.
- **The hour before kick-off**: the game already takes its place, with 0 - 0 and a countdown to the kick-off.
- **Earlier**: both teams' records, the day and the kick-off time in your bar's time zone. **Afterwards**: Final (Final/OT after overtime) and the score, the loser dimmed. A game that is postponed or cancelled says so; a game stopped by the weather shows Delay.
- **When the season is over**: for 30 days after the title game, a champions' banner takes turns with that game's final score: gold rules, confetti, a trophy and the winner's helmet. After that the card reads SEASON IS OVER / OFFSEASON, with the days to the next kick-off once the schedule is known.
- Each team is a small helmet in its colours over its three letters. The app carries no league or team logos.

While a game is on, the app stays on one game: your team's if it is on, otherwise the first one. When no game is on, the finished games and the ones to come take turns, six seconds each.

## Controls

- **Wheel**: the next or the previous game that is on.
- **OK** (press the wheel): jump to your team's game. If it is not on, a card says so.
- **Start**: a look around the league: the other games that are on, the finished ones, the ones to come, and back to your game. Start again goes straight back.
- **Back**: exit.

## Settings

- **My team**: one of the 32 teams. The app starts on its game, and OK jumps to it.
- **Big play effects**: the signs for touchdowns and the like, on or off.

A change applies within 10 seconds; no restart is needed.

## Data and privacy

- Scores come from ESPN's public scoreboard endpoints (site.api.espn.com, site.web.api.espn.com and sports.core.api.espn.com), read by the bar itself over Wi-Fi. They are unofficial, need no key and no account, and can change or stop without notice. Live numbers run some 10 to 30 seconds behind the stadium.
- The app sends no personal data: only plain requests for the week's games and, after the season, for the title game and the date the next season starts. On the bar it keeps two small files: the list of this week's games and a count of failed requests.
- This app is not affiliated with or endorsed by the NFL, its teams or ESPN. Team names and abbreviations are used only to say who is playing.

## Without a connection

- The last numbers stay on screen, and the app asks again after 10, 20, 40 seconds and so on, up to every 2 minutes. A live game whose numbers are more than two and a half minutes old shows a grey quarter and --:-- for the clock.
- If nothing could be loaded at all, a card says why: NO WI-FI when the bar is offline, NO DATA / RETRYING when it is online and the hosts do not answer.
- In a week without games during the season the card reads NO GAMES / THIS WEEK.
